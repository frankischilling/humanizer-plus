# Wikipedia guide audit

This audit compares Humanizer 4.0.0 at [upstream commit b48c832](https://github.com/blader/humanizer/tree/b48c832f0f809c1b5658edbb838c2521b72f2413) with the pinned [Signs of AI writing guide](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?oldid=1386293299) and its companion pages on [style](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?oldid=1385890225), [markup](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?oldid=1385885300), and [comments](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?oldid=1386264853). The comparison covers the guide's caveats, current and historical indicators, and linked treatments of style, markup, and comments. Pattern numbers below refer to Humanizer Plus 4.1.0.

## Coverage

| Guide area | What Humanizer Plus does |
|---|---|
| Detection tools and human judgment | Uses patterns to find editing problems. A reader's impression and a detector score cannot establish authorship. |
| Significance, legacy, and wider trends | §13 already covered inflated impact and stock outlooks. It now covers unsupported claims about sparking a wider debate. Keep documented legacy, specific challenges, and real plans. |
| Notability, media coverage, primary and tertiary sources, and online presence | §17 already covered prestige lists and vague sources. It now addresses needless narration of what a website, catalog, or database calls someone. Keep material attribution and relevant coverage or audience figures. |
| Superficial analysis and unclear relationships | §14 and §15 cover both. Keep a real relationship or analysis, and distinguish the writer's interpretation from a source's account. |
| Vague attribution and overstated agreement | §17 now explicitly preserves which sources expressed an opinion and how many did. Unsupported generalizations need a question for the author. |
| Awards and recognition | §13 and §17 address sections padded with unnamed honors, press mentions, or generic praise. A real award can have its own section if it serves readers. |
| Stock vocabulary, substitutes for simple verbs, negative parallelism, and triads | §1, §6, §12, and §18 cover these. Clusters and rhetorical function matter more than isolated words. A necessary contrast or a list of three real items stays. |
| List titles treated as names | §24 now covers leads that define a descriptive headline as though it named an entity. Keep genuine names and useful scope sentences. |
| Bold, inline labels, heading levels, title case, emoji, tables, and quotes | §19–§21 cover presentation. §20 now includes hierarchy and unnecessary tables. The destination determines normal heading levels and whether title case or curly punctuation fits. |
| Chat wrappers and draft advice | §22 now covers author-facing notes and publishing instructions seen in pasted drafts, including hidden comments. Keep genuine disclosures and useful file comments. |
| Source caveats and disambiguation | §23 now covers needless warnings about how readers should interpret a source. Keep a relevant evidence limit, distinction, or qualifier. |
| Template prose, date and URL placeholders | New §26 covers unfinished slots in deliverables. Reusable templates and intentional example addresses keep their slots. |
| Markdown in wikitext, broken syntax, internal tags, categories, and templates | New §27 treats these as destination or rendering problems. Real code examples, valid Markdown, and functional wiki metadata belong where readers need them. |
| Dead links, invalid identifiers, unrelated DOIs, and book locators | New §28 covers source identity and claim-level support. A failed lookup can come from a typo, link decay, limited access, or an unrelated citation. Add a locator only when verified. |
| Reference syntax and unused named references | §27 and §28 apply when the destination calls for those conventions. In wikitext, compare a named reference's definition and uses against the page. |
| Tracking tags and search URLs | §28 treats them as reasons to inspect what the link actually supports. A tracking parameter says little about accuracy. A search URL can be useful in instructions and inadequate as evidence for a fact. |
| Discussion comments | §5, §22, §25, and §29 cover canned assurances, overjustification, repeated policy language, and generic offers. Keep concrete requests, meaningful courtesies, and accurate policy references. |
| Edit summaries | §25 now covers stock claims about review feedback, policy compliance, preservation, source quality, and incidental markup. State the actual change. Include a template name, citation, or policy when it explains the change. |
| Style shifts, submission pitches, maintenance tags, and canned profile pages | A change of audience or speaker can explain a shift. §20, §22, and §25 address generic profile copy and review pitches. §27 flags markup for review. Wikipedia's review and maintenance labels depend on its workflow. |
| Editing conduct, model differences, and content bias | Account activity and permission changes are outside a prose edit. Model-specific style varies. Flag unsupported generalizations and gaps in evidence without guessing a model or substituting the editor's views. |
| Age, editorial explanations, syntax, and ineffective indicators | The skill keeps ordinary syntax, author explanations, and deliberate voice. Text dates, good grammar, academic register, mixed registers, missing sources, or unusual wiki syntax alone do not warrant rewriting or an authorship claim. |
| Historical promotional language, knowledge cutoffs, summaries, and prompt refusals | §2, §16, and §22–§23 treat these as editing issues when they are out of place. Keep the genre's intended enthusiasm, useful summaries, and real disclosures. |
| Historical dashes, cutoffs, access dates, and unnecessary synonym changes | §8 drops the blanket dash ban. Flag a visibly incomplete passage and any date that needs checking. Keep deliberate variation, typographic ranges, and valid access dates. |

The guide's reference list, further reading, and external links provide background for its observations. Wikipedia's rules for article eligibility, user permissions, and conduct apply to its editors. For a wiki task, check the policy and the scope of the requested edit before applying a wiki-specific change.
